# AISG My Courses — Learning Quality, Cultural Responsiveness & TLF Audit

**Audit date:** September 2026  
**Scope:** All 16 courses currently published in AISG My Courses  
**Purpose:** Review the catalogue as professional learning — not only as content — for conceptual accuracy, depth of thinking, transfer to practice, cultural responsiveness in AISG's Guangzhou context, and coherent use of the AISG Transformative Learning Framework (TLF).

## Quality standard used

Every course was reviewed against the following questions:

1. **Purpose and conceptual precision** — Is the course teaching a defensible professional idea rather than a collection of tips or rules?
2. **Grounding and epistemic clarity** — Is it clear whether the learning is based on AISG policy/handbook expectations, an AISG framework, an external research framework, or a professional synthesis?
3. **Cognitive demand** — Do checks progress beyond recall toward application, analysis, professional judgement and synthesis?
4. **Transfer** — Does the course help a faculty member make a better decision in practice rather than simply complete a quiz?
5. **Cultural responsiveness** — Does the learning avoid deficit assumptions and cultural stereotypes, value multilingual and culturally diverse ways of knowing, and make room for AISG's local Guangzhou / southern China context as well as wider international perspectives?
6. **TLF coherence** — Where a connection is genuine, does the course use the shared language of Personalisation, Agency, Authenticity, Creativity, Taking Action and Collaboration accurately? Where the connection is weak or inappropriate, is the framework left out rather than forced?
7. **Learner evidence** — Does the course keep attention on what students experience, say, choose, create, connect, do and become, rather than treating adult activity as evidence of learning?
8. **Professional humility** — Does the course encourage evidence, inquiry and reflection before judgement, especially when language, culture, identity, family context or learner difference may shape what is observed?

## Cross-catalogue changes made

A new **AISG learning lens** is now visible on the home page of every shared-engine course. It makes three things explicit before the learner begins:

- the **grounding** of the course;
- the **cultural-responsiveness lens** most relevant to that content; and
- the **TLF connection**, including when alignment should remain secondary rather than be forced.

Selected sections also include a short **Deepen the lens** note where the audit identified a high-value misconception, contextual issue or cross-framework connection. These additions are deliberately concise; they deepen professional judgement without turning each course into a longer theory module.

## Course-by-course findings

### Safeguarding at AISG

**Status:** Strong; no forced TLF alignment added.

This course is correctly policy- and procedure-first. The strongest design choice is that it teaches thresholds, disclosure response, reporting, professional boundaries and student welfare through realistic safeguarding decisions rather than treating safeguarding as a generic wellbeing topic. Cultural difference must never be used as a reason to delay action when there is reasonable cause, while family structures, caregiving arrangements and communication norms should not themselves be treated as evidence of harm. The existing course already reinforces neutral clarification, the student's own words, confidentiality and prompt reporting. TLF language is intentionally not used as a decision framework here because safeguarding requirements govern the response.

### Elementary Faculty Essentials

**Status:** Strong and appropriately localised.

The course already uses multilingual/home-language scenarios, equitable rather than identical support, caregiver privacy, developmentally appropriate learning, inclusive support and current AISG/Guangzhou transition contexts. The new learning lens clarifies that TLF language can strengthen reflection on learner experience but does not replace Elementary, PYP or handbook requirements.

### Secondary Faculty Essentials

**Status:** Strong and appropriately localised.

The course already includes multilingual access, home-language sense-making, Chinese history/culture within experiential learning, Guangzhou community transition, programme pathways, evidence-informed grading, sensitive topics and professional judgement. The new lens reinforces that one communication style, prior-school norm or English proficiency level should not become the default standard for interpreting learner engagement.

### Employee Communication Guidelines

**Status:** Strong; strengthened cultural-humility framing.

The policy logic is excellent: Purpose → Audience → Information → Evidence → Language → Confidentiality → Professionalism. The audit identified one important interpretive risk: factual language can still be distorted by culturally shaped assumptions. A new learning note therefore asks faculty to distinguish observable patterns from judgements based on eye contact, silence, directness, public disagreement, turn-taking, English fluency or family communication conventions. This strengthens the existing 'Describe, Don't Label' principle without changing communication policy.

### AI in Education

**Status:** Strong; strengthened local-context verification.

The course already teaches AI literacy, hallucination/verification, privacy, human oversight, bias, authentic evidence of learning and purposeful TLF-aligned use. The audit added an explicit local-relevance principle: a generated answer can be factually plausible yet still be unsuitable for Guangzhou because policies, services, platforms, examples, histories or cultural assumptions do not transfer cleanly. This adds cultural and contextual verification to factual verification.

### Assessment for Learning at AISG

**Status:** Strong; construct-validity lens strengthened.

The course is conceptually sound because it defines formative assessment by the use of evidence rather than by task labels. It also treats feedback as incomplete until learners act on it. The new note makes construct validity more explicit: when language, speed or response format is not the intended construct, those features should not quietly dominate the inference; when they are part of the construct, they should remain while access around them is scaffolded. This strengthens equity without lowering expectations.

### Data to Action: Using Evidence to Improve Learning

**Status:** Strong; interpretation lens strengthened.

The current-state → desired-state → gap → action → progress-monitoring → adjust cycle is coherent and disciplined. The course already warns against deficit interpretations of multilingual participation. The audit added the principle that numerical data is not automatically culturally or linguistically neutral: language demands, examples, timing, response conventions, opportunity to learn and access can affect what a measure validly captures. Disaggregation should create better questions, not more precise labels.

### Designing for Learner Variability

**Status:** Very strong.

The UDL course correctly reflects CAST UDL 3.0, rejects learning-styles matching, separates goals from non-essential means, preserves meaningful challenge, and treats identity, belonging, bias and representation as design issues rather than learner deficits. It already aligns naturally with TLF Personalisation and Agency. No substantive rewrite was warranted; the new course-home lens clarifies that variability exists within as well as between cultural/linguistic groups, helping prevent identity from becoming a stereotype about preference.

### Engagement for All: The AISG Learning Framework

**Status:** Very strong; cultural responsiveness made more explicit as cross-cutting.

The course accurately teaches the four layers of the TLF and repeatedly states that the framework is a lens, not a checklist. A new learning note makes explicit that **Cultural Responsiveness is not a seventh facet**. It is expressed through whose languages, identities and strengths shape Personalisation; whose perspectives influence Agency and Authenticity; whose histories and communities matter in Taking Action; and whether Collaboration genuinely values difference. The note also emphasises that local Guangzhou / Pearl River Delta contexts can be deeply authentic without needing a distant 'global' framing.

### Multi-Tiered System of Supports (MTSS)

**Status:** Strong; multilingual / learning-difference interpretation strengthened.

The course already teaches MTSS as a prevention system rather than a triangle, and treats screening as a signal rather than a diagnosis. The audit added a dual-lens reminder: for multilingual learners, difficulty may reflect language development, instructional or assessment design, a learning difference, several factors together, or something else. High-consequence attribution should use evidence across modes, language-proficiency information, response to appropriate scaffolds, learner/family knowledge and EAL/Learning Support expertise.

### Supporting Multilingual Learners

**Status:** Strong and highly relevant to AISG.

The course is asset-based, separates English proficiency from intelligence/content knowledge, integrates content and language, teaches purposeful translanguaging, scaffolding, interaction and collaborative evidence use. The audit added a safeguard against essentialising identity: learners may use English, Mandarin, Cantonese and/or other home languages differently, and should be invited — not required — to draw on cultural and linguistic experience. No learner should be positioned as the representative of an entire culture or language community.

### Technology for Transformative Learning

**Status:** Strong; important AISG systems alignment added.

The course already makes the central distinction that technology is not a seventh TLF facet and should amplify a desired learner experience rather than simply digitise activity. The audit added the **SMART-T** lens for PLCs considering regular digital-tool adoption: Student Learning Need; Model of Use; Accessibility & Safety; Resources & Readiness; Teaching & Curriculum Alignment. This makes an important distinction between 'good use in one lesson' and 'responsible, sustainable adoption'. For Guangzhou, practical fitness also includes reliable access, language accessibility, privacy/data protection, technical compatibility and support capacity.

### Teacher Growth & Reflection — Domain 1

**Status:** Very strong; directly continuum-grounded.

The course accurately uses the five-level AISG Teacher Growth Continuum and centres learner evidence rather than teacher self-labelling. It already addresses learner identity, language, multiple pathways, culturally sustaining task design, assessment for growth and increasing learner independence. No substantial rewrite was warranted.

### Teacher Growth & Reflection — Domain 2

**Status:** Very strong; directly continuum-grounded.

The course appropriately develops belonging, voice, advocacy, autonomy, routines and purposeful use of learning environments. The audit reinforces that psychological safety does not require one culturally preferred communication style: different legitimate routes into contribution can coexist with shared expectations for dignity and learning.

### Teacher Growth & Reflection — Domain 3

**Status:** Very strong; local authenticity strengthened.

The course is already the catalogue's clearest expression of transformative and culturally responsive learning: cognitive demand, inclusive discourse, diverse perspectives, meaningful inquiry, creativity, authentic application and social impact. A new note makes local authenticity explicit: school, neighbourhood, Guangzhou and Pearl River Delta questions can provide rigorous authentic contexts and then connect outward to national/global patterns where that strengthens the inquiry.

### Teacher Growth & Reflection — Domain 4

**Status:** Very strong; partnership framing clarified.

The course appropriately broadens the lens from individual classroom practice to feedback, evidence, learner reflection, professional collaboration, collective capacity and community partnership. The new course-home lens reinforces reciprocal partnership: multilingual family/community knowledge should inform professional understanding rather than communication being treated only as information sent outward from school.

## TLF alignment principle used in this audit

The TLF was **not** treated as a branding requirement. Alignment was added only when it clarifies the learner experience or strengthens a professional decision.

- Strong/direct alignment: Engagement for All; Technology; Teacher Growth Domains; parts of UDL, Assessment, Multilingual Learning, AI and Data.
- Useful but secondary alignment: Faculty Essentials, Communication and MTSS.
- Intentionally not used as the governing framework: Safeguarding, where policy, professional duty and reporting procedures take precedence.

This protects the TLF from becoming a compliance checklist and keeps the language meaningful.

## Cultural responsiveness principle used in this audit

'Culturally responsive' does not mean adding stereotypical references to China or asking students to represent a nationality. Across the catalogue it means:

- treating language, identity and lived experience as assets;
- checking whose knowledge and perspectives are present or absent;
- distinguishing cultural difference from deficit;
- examining whether participation and assessment conventions privilege one communication norm;
- using local knowledge, people, histories, environments and community questions when they create meaningful authenticity;
- designing communication and partnership so multilingual learners and families can contribute knowledge, not only receive information;
- maintaining rigorous expectations while changing unnecessary barriers;
- remaining curious about context before drawing a high-consequence conclusion.

## Remaining governance boundary

The catalogue now has a stronger and more explicit quality/context layer, but professional-learning synthesis should still receive the relevant human content-owner review before institutional assignment. Policy and handbook courses must continue to be updated from the authoritative source documents when those sources change. Draft inclusion-review recommendations remain developmental input rather than formal AISG policy unless and until they are adopted through the appropriate process.
